# Opportunity research: Qloo agentic product

Research date: October 3, 2026. Desk research only. No customer interviews, outreach, user tests, API experiments, or willingness-to-pay observations were conducted. Qualitative judgments below are hypotheses, not a validated startup rating.

## Verdict

The strongest **testable eight-week hypothesis** is an operator planning agent for **independent paid local dinner or cultural communities that already select outside venues for recurring, known opt-in groups of 4–8**. Call the working concept Common Ground. The host supplies the group; the product helps select a feasible venue, understand compromises, handle vetoes, and produce an approved plan. Do not enter guest matchmaking, ticketing, consumer acquisition, or reservation inventory in the first version.

This is a narrower, more reachable workflow than a general travel recommender. It is **not a proven business**, and the research does not justify “9/10.” Paid operators plausibly have an incentive to save planning time, but neither their pain intensity nor incremental software budget is established. If discovery reveals they use the same few restaurants, primarily need more ticket buyers, or will not ask guests for cultural preferences, abandon this wedge.

NYC is an initial geographic assumption because several observed examples operate there. Replace it with a city where the founder can access operators and verify venues. Select one compact neighborhood/catchment before trying citywide coverage.

## Evidence standard

- **Direct operational evidence:** a business's own live offering, event calendar, priced service, or named practitioner description. Establishes that a workflow exists; does not establish demand for this product.
- **Named customer testimony hosted by a vendor:** more concrete than generic marketing, but selected by the seller; no independent causal verification.
- **Primary research:** original sector studies. Useful for problem context; does not prove a particular solution.
- **Seller capability claim:** useful for competitive coverage; not treated as independent evidence of efficacy.
- **Our inference:** buyer, go-to-market, scope, ranking, and proposed tests. Must be validated.

## 1. Paid local small-group community planning

**What is actually observed.** [table for one(s)](https://forones.co/) advertises 4–6-person curated dinners for solo visitors in NYC, with Thursday and Sunday offerings at named restaurants and observed prices of $50 and $65. This proves a paid recurring small-table service exists, not its revenue, profitability, or need for planning SaaS. It also uses visitors rather than repeat members, so repeat-exclusion features may be less relevant there.

[Table 315](https://www.table315.com/) describes monthly dinner clubs in Central New York, 4–6-person tables across different restaurants, a quiz, dietary inputs, and location reveal. It is a useful example of an independent operator, but the advertised monthly cadence **does not meet** the proposed minimum of two outings/month. Its dietary example is not evidence of medically reliable allergy verification.

[New York Dinner Club's Meetup listing](https://www.meetup.com/meetup-group-yyedoizw/) describes 4–6 people meeting at local spots for brunch, dinner, or coffee. It is an organizer's self-description, not customer proof. [Meetup's event documentation](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/39790436736525-Creating-an-event) supports venues, event fees, attendee limits, questions, and recurrence. This demonstrates incumbent workflow coverage, not a missing venue optimizer.

**Recommended exact screener.** An independent operator already runs at least two outside-venue dinners/cultural outings a month; typically 4–8 opt-in attendees per group; selects among multiple venues; earns event/membership fees or has an explicit operating budget; and can personally authorize a pilot. The product receives already formed groups. Anonymous first-time attendees can participate, but no claim about repeated compromise should be made until repeat attendance exists.

**Reachability inference.** Small operators publish events and contact routes, so prospect identification can be done manually with no paid lead database. This is not evidence that they will answer, buy, or have enough volume. No outreach was sent.

**Qloo's indispensable hypothesis.** Guests give a small set of optional artist, film, book, brand, or place seeds. Qloo translates them into a structured cross-domain venue ordering. The application aggregates individual orderings, surfaces who bears the largest compromise, and lets explicit feedback change the plan. Affinity is a recommendation signal—not an enjoyment probability, interpersonal compatibility score, personality inference, or allergy/accessibility fact. If Qloo does not beat a good explicit-preference baseline, the intended differentiator fails.

**Killing test.** Have real hosts bring their last three planning decisions. If most use a fixed venue roster without meaningful comparison, cannot name costly rework, or cannot recruit enough guests to provide opt-in signals, do not proceed. Then compare Qloo against the host's shortlist and a practical baseline under identical constraints; if guests and hosts cannot see a useful difference, the Qloo-dependent product case is weak.

## 2. Cultural programming / independent venue audience development

[NIVA's original State of Live study announcement](https://www.nivassoc.org/statements/2025/independent-stages-generate-1531-billion-in-economic-investment-across-us-based-on-new-national-independent-venue-association-niva-research-study) reports that 64% of independent stages in its 2024 study were unprofitable and attributes pressure to multiple sector forces. This is trade-association-sponsored research conducted with TEConomy, not a finding that poor recommendations cause venue losses. Its population spans venues, festivals, and promoters, so do not apply the statistic to every target venue.

[Wallace / University of Texas research](https://wallacefoundation.org/report/search-magic-bullet-results-building-audiences-sustainability-initiative-results-building) studied audience-building at established nonprofits and found engaging audiences takes time and may not work on organizations' preferred terms. Its methods included extensive staff interviews; its institutional context does not automatically generalize to a tiny club. [A separate Wallace review](https://wallacefoundation.org/report/audience-building-and-financial-health-nonprofit-arts?p=1) warns that growing audiences may not improve finances and that approaches lack an obvious universal solution.

Named marketing workflows are tangible: [Mailchimp's MCA Denver case](https://mailchimp.com/resources/mcadenver-boosted-email-performance/) identifies a marketing manager and an organization running over 100 programs/events annually. This is a vendor-hosted customer account. [The Audience Agency](https://theaudienceagency.org/en/news/audience-spectrum-now-digs-deeper) supplies cultural segmentation, drawing on cultural consumption and organizations' data. That is relevant prior art, not a freely accessible audience database for the proposed product.

**Best version of this wedge:** take one confirmed upcoming show and its artist seeds, identify a few adjacent cultural interests and *specific public local partners* the venue can actually approach, and create a reviewable promotion plan. Starting with programming choices introduces artist availability, fees, rights, production costs, and calendars that Qloo does not solve.

**Qloo lift:** compare partner relevance and attributed ticket conversions from a small real campaign against the venue's existing partner plan, holding offer/creative/channel approximately constant. Without operator access to a list/campaign or an agreement to track conversions, the demo only measures plausibility—not revenue lift.

**Killing test:** marketing leads prefer better email/ticketing operations, have no channel through which to act on inferred interests, or treat recommended partners as already known. A list of culturally related people is not distribution.

## 3. Boutique hotel concierge

[Mindtrip for Hotels](https://mindtrip.ai/business/hotels) already offers property content indexing, hotel team curated guides, trip planning, QR/email/SMS distribution, and engagement reporting. Its named Landsby marketing-director testimonial says a small hospitality team implemented it and eased front-desk volume. This is direct buyer testimony selected and hosted by the vendor, not an independently measured ROI study.

[HiJiffy's Kabannas case](https://www.hijiffy.com/success-stories/kabannas) describes a three-hotel group paying for a Premium plan and operational use across guest communications. [Duve's Edgar Suites case](https://duve.com/how-edgar-suites-leverages-duveai-to-automate-80-of-guest-inquiries/) describes guest communication/check-in automation and a named guest-satisfaction lead. These show real hotel teams buy software, but the documented job centers on operational questions and integrated messaging—not evidence of willingness to pay specifically for mixed-taste offsite recommendations.

[Qloo's recommendation offering](https://www.qloo.com/capabilities/recommendations) already markets travel personalization and a Michelin customer outcome. Treat the reported booking lift as Qloo's vendor case claim; do not transfer it to a new boutique-hotel product or infer the experiment design from the headline.

**Counterargument:** selling a narrow taste layer into an established concierge category requires proving why a hotel should add another workflow or why an existing vendor cannot add Qloo. The manager may also prefer their established commission/referral partners. A standalone opt-in guide can be built in eight weeks, but hotel procurement and proof of financial return need longer.

**Killing test:** operators want FAQ automation/PMS integration before they will pilot, or there are too few group-venue requests to justify a new tool. Do not assume access to reservations, guest records, OTA chat, property management systems, or commission agreements.

## 4. Novel commercial alternative: cultural sponsorship prospecting

The useful buyer is a local chamber/festival/arts sponsorship seller, not a consumer. The job is to find suitable prospects outside the same habitual local sponsors, substantiate fit, and save research time before outreach.

[Greater Manchester Chamber's own Taco Tour announcement](https://manchester-chamber.org/taco-tour-manchester-2025-date-announced/) names Casamigos as presenting sponsor, contains a brand-side marketing VP comment, and identifies the sponsorship contact. That is direct transaction/category evidence. [SponsorPitch's chamber page](https://www.sponsorpitch.com/discover-sponsorpitch-cc) reports a named Taco Tour customer result. Its dollar/ROI claims are vendor-hosted; the chamber source corroborates sponsorship activity, not the amount or the tool's causal impact.

[SponsorPitch's product](https://www.sponsorpitch.com/how-it-works) combines sponsorship activity research, decision-maker contacts, a pitch marketplace, and CRM support. [SponsorUnited's performing-arts page](https://www.sponsorunited.com/insights/building-meaningful-sponsorships-in-the-performing-arts-space) explicitly discusses sponsorship packages, niche reach, and ROI challenges. These are competing sellers' descriptions.

Qloo could supply an unusual brand-fit ordering from event artists/titles and geography. A real agent must then separate inferred affinity from publicly observed sponsorship activity, fit to a package, and an appropriate verified contact route. [Qloo's Netflix merchandising case](https://www.qloo.com/resources/netflix-and-qloo-elevate-merchandising-with-ai-driven-audience-insights) describes adjacent brand partnerships, so cross-domain commercial fit is also part of Qloo's own proposition.

**Counterargument:** an attractive affinity shortlist can still be commercially useless. Sponsor intent, decision-maker access, budgets, exclusivity, existing agreements, timing, and brand restrictions may determine sales. Free public data cannot reproduce incumbents' contacts/deal databases. Selling research may be more viable than SaaS initially; seasonal campaigns also weaken month-to-month recurrence.

**Killing test:** sellers reject culturally unusual prospects because they lack spend evidence, or most recommendations are unactionable without a paid contacts database. Measure accepted *new, actionable* prospects and research time first; closed deals take longer and cannot be claimed after a demo.

## 5. Coworking: a conditional channel, not the default buyer

[A named NUMA community-manager interview on Cobot](https://blog.cobot.me/coworking-at-numa-bengaluru/) establishes internal and external community events as real work. [Everything Coworking's practitioner article](https://www.everythingcoworking.com/everything-coworking-blog/5-steps-to-creating-a-coworking-member-event-program) recounts poor attendance at a 1 pm event and using a free Google Form to gather member input. This is relevant first-hand practitioner context, not a controlled study or proof of offsite venue-selection demand.

[Coworks' problem article](https://www.coworks.com/blog/top-5-coworking-community-manager-problems) claims event coordination/promotion are burdens; it is vendor marketing. [Zoku's own community-dinner listing](https://livezoku.com/paris/events/community-dinner-members-only/) shows recurring dinners hosted by a community manager **at its own property**. This is a concrete reason not to equate event recurrence with recurring external venue searches.

Keep coworking operators only when they pass the same offsite frequency, group-size, venue-comparison, and budget screener. Do not imply event attendance or member retention improves because Qloo is used.

## Evaluation that could establish a real Qloo contribution

Use three arms with the same city, candidate pool, explicit constraints, sources, and explanation quality: (A) host's current process; (B) explicit venue preferences/constraints plus a competent LLM or simple ranker; (C) the same workflow with Qloo cultural seeds. Capture pre-explanation choices where possible so persuasive copy does not masquerade as better recommendations.

Measure planning minutes, feasible choices in the shortlist, guest response/completion, host-approved plans, veto/replan cycles, and blind guest preference between shortlists. Log missing-source constraints and rejection causes. Qloo ranks are not commensurable calibrated utilities across people; evaluate an ordinal aggregation policy and show its limitations. “No one repeatedly receives their worst option” is a policy goal to test, not a promised emotional outcome.

Do not claim statistical significance from a handful of groups. Descriptive pilot results and transparent failures are appropriate. Attendance, repeat purchase, and retention are later business outcomes with many confounders. No numeric improvement target is an existing result.
